# Synthesis

You write these yourself, from `research/` and the verified ledger. Agents supply evidence and you make the argument. Read every `section.md` in full. Cite with `[c:id]` on every number, date and named claim. Use only claims with a verdict of confirmed or partly, or plainly labelled inference.

Keep three things apart: the overview says what is, the so what says what it means, and the moves say what to do. Read `references/moves.md` and `references/insights.md` first. S3b has already gathered their evidence.

## 03-overview.md: status

- The company in one paragraph.
- Key numbers table: metric, value, as-of date, fact or inference.
- How it got here, phase by phase, in order.
- How it makes money today.
- How it grows today.
- Team and funding.
- Main risks, as facts.
- File index, reading rules, method, data cutoff, and what could not be established.

No recommendations here.

## 04-so-what.md: meaning

**Part A: the company on its own.**
- Verdict in three sentences.
- Why it works, and what the evidence says drove that. Separate cause from coincidence.
- What is fragile.
- Lessons that travel, each with the evidence and when it would not apply.
- Risks ranked by likelihood and damage.
- What would change this reading.
- The strongest alternative reading, from the synthesis review.

**Part B: for the decision in the brief.** What it means for that decision, the options, the call, and what would flip it. Action advice for the reader belongs in `09-moves.md`, so Part B stays short and points there.

## 05-gtm.md

The engine in order: channels, formula, cadence, what each action moved, funnel and loops, and what is measured. Say what is reachable and what is unknown. End with what is copyable and what depends on assets others lack, such as a founder's audience.

## 06-competition.md

Bottom line first. Map of groups, head-to-head table, threats and partners with reasons, patterns across players, substitutes, and gaps. Scores are judgement, so show the rubric.

## 07-deep-dive.md

An index to the depth: the most useful findings from each workstream that did not fit above, with links to its section. Economics worked example, legal exposure, product or technical structure, team history.

## 08-decision-memo.md

One page, for someone who reads nothing else:
1. The question.
2. The answer.
3. Three to five reasons, each with a citation.
4. What would change it.
5. **The cheapest real-world test of the answer.**
6. **Who to speak to and what to ask:** types of people (customers, former staff, partners, vendors), with five questions each. These fill what public research cannot reach. Nobody is contacted by the run.
7. How much to trust this: the verification label and the interval.

## 09-moves.md

Follow `references/moves.md`. It opens the page. The memo's answer and the moves' answer must agree.

## 09-lens.md (only if the brief named one)

Apply the findings to the user's question. Keep it separate from the company research, and label what is carried over and what is assumed. Research for a lens runs as its own brief, after G2.

## Before showing the user

- Re-read the original ask in `00-brief.md`. Did this answer it?
- Re-check all math.
- Run `references/style.md` over it.
- Remove anything that doesn't help the reader decide.
