# Cold-Eye Recipient Review

Use this skill when reviewing any work product about to leave your hands — going to a client, partner, prospect, public audience, or anyone other than yourself.

This is a **Pass 2**: it reviews how the work *lands*, not whether it's *correct*. Correctness is Pass 1 and comes first.

## Pre-condition: Pass 1 must be clean before invoking

Before running the recipient review, confirm the deliverable has passed a correctness check:
- All names, numbers, dates, claims, sources, and product names fact-checked
- Sources cited inline (URLs > paraphrase) for anything non-obvious, quantitative, time-sensitive, or contested
- No fabricated citations
- Math re-checked
- The work answers the actual question asked, not an adjacent one

If Pass 1 is not clean, stop and run it first. Don't run the recipient review on work that's still factually shaky — a great-landing email with a wrong number is worse than a clunky one that's correct.

## Pass 2: The recipient-POV review

### Step 1 — Gather recipient context
Use whatever you have on the recipient: prior call transcripts (Fireflies, Otter, or similar), past emails, relationship history, their role, their stated priorities, how they read and react. If you have no context, say so and ask the user for a quick brief before continuing — don't guess.

### Step 2 — Read end-to-end as the recipient
Read with the recipient's eyes, not the writer's. Catch:
- **Tone friction** — too formal, too casual, off-key for the relationship
- **Missing pieces they'd expect** — greeting, context, next step, deadline, attachment reference
- **Confusion points** — jargon they don't share, references they don't have
- **Implicit asks** — things you didn't say but they'll feel
- **Off-brand bits** — anything that doesn't sound like the sender

### Step 3 — Report how it lands in 3 levels of depth
This is the deliverable back to the user. Format:

**Skim impression (5 seconds, scanning):**
What's the recipient's gut reaction in their first 5 seconds? Subject line, first paragraph, headline — what do they see?

**Mid-depth read (1-2 minutes, paying attention):**
What's their working understanding after reading it once? What do they take as the message, the ask, the tone?

**Deepest interpretation (3-5 minutes, reading between lines):**
What would they really conclude? What might they suspect? What might they push back on? What does it tell them about the sender as a counterparty / consultant / collaborator?

### Step 4 — Iterate
If any level surfaces something the user would want fixed before sending, fix it first, then tell them what you changed and why. Loop until the deliverable is at "I'd send this as-is" quality.

### Step 5 — For high-stakes external work
When the work is high-stakes (first impression with a new client, paid deliverable, public-facing, irreversible) — spawn a separate reviewer subagent with the recipient context loaded fresh, and have them red-team the deliverable independently before showing the user. Second pair of eyes on critical work.

## "I'd send this as-is" means

Pass 2 clears when:
- All three depth-levels report a positive landing
- No tone friction left
- No missing pieces the recipient would expect
- No off-key bits
- The recipient would respond the way the sender wants them to

If Pass 2 doesn't clear after 2-3 iterations, **stop and tell the user what's blocking** — don't keep grinding silently.
