# Writing and claim rules

Defaults that came out of two real B2B page builds. If the user has house rules of their own, they go into `decisions.md` and win over these.

## Words
- Plain words, short sentences. Read every line as a stranger arriving from a messaging app: no "they" or "it" without context, no insider terms, no process jargon in the first screen.
- No em-dashes, no exclamation marks, no "it's not X, it's Y" or "not just X but Y" phrasing. These read as machine-written and cost trust.
- Labels match reality: "two quick questions" means two.
- Buttons say exactly what happens ("Request your start", "Reserve my spot").

## A sales page, not a contract
- Keep it simple and directionally right. Result numbers carry one plain label for their period ("Month 1") and no footnotes, averages, sample sizes or time windows. Caveats make a page timid and cost more conversions than they protect.
- Flag only claims that would be plainly false: an invented number, client, result or testimonial, or a result stated as something it was not (one post shown as the average, a team's result shown as one person's, someone else's result under the seller's name). Say it once; the seller decides.
- Operational detail stays off the page: payment terms, schedules, cancellation rules, legal entity details, VAT amounts (one short line such as "Prices exclude VAT" at most). It belongs in the order confirmation.

## Words that shrink or frighten
- Lock-in ("locked for 12 months", "minimum term"): if there is a first term, state it once, plainly, next to the prices.
- Just starting out ("founding member", "beta").
- Confidentiality risk ("built from client work").
- Proof framed as "my best result": show it as before and after instead.

## Proof
- Before and after side by side, the same rows, real numbers, a multiplier where the numbers allow.
- Real people make proof checkable: names, photos, profile links.
- When no client proof exists yet, a clearly labelled example of the product's output in the buyer's world shows what they would get without claiming a client.

## The reader is the reasonable one
The surprise is the system around them, not their failure. The seller appears as someone who learned something, not as the hero.
