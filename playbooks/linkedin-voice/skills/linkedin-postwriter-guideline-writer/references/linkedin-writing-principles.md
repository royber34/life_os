# LinkedIn writing principles (the universal layer)

Inject these into the guideline as the supporting layer under the person's voice. If a principle conflicts with their real voice, the voice wins and you note the exception.

The engagement stats below come from creator and marketing-firm analyses (for example Richard van der Blom's annual report), not LinkedIn's official disclosures. Mark them directional, not gospel.

## 1. The hook is the whole game
LinkedIn truncates the post at roughly 140 characters on mobile (most traffic) before the "see more" cut. Nothing downstream matters if line 1 does not earn the click. Design the first 1 to 2 lines for that budget.

## 2. Lead with the felt outcome, not an insider metric
Open on a result or pain the reader recognizes from their own experience. A number only an expert can interpret is not a hook; the felt consequence is. Numbers are proof, placed after the hook.

## 3. Optimize for dwell time and comments, not likes
Dwell time and substantive comments drive distribution far more than likes. The first 1 to 2 hours matter most. Write for a reply worth making.

## 4. No links in the post body
An external link in the body cuts reach materially. Put the link in the first comment, or use a "comment X and I'll send it" mechanic. (LinkedIn now also dampens comment links somewhat, so a post that stands alone is best.) When using a comment-for-link CTA, end on it: one ask, not two.

## 5. Length
Aim for roughly 1,200 to 1,900 characters for substantive posts. Long enough to deliver value and dwell time, short enough to finish. Very short "quick takes" tend to underperform substantive ones.

## 6. Formatting: whitespace is a feature
Short paragraphs (1 to 2 lines), generous line breaks, scannable. No walls of text. Bullets lower perceived effort, but only when there are genuine points to list.

## 7. Structures (pick one per post)
Personal story; listicle; contrarian take; teach / how-to; observation; build-in-public / teardown / case study. Match the structure to the person's strongest register.

## 8. Hashtags: minimal
About 3 relevant tags at most. Topical relevance in the body matters more than tags. Do not invent trendy tags the person does not use.

## 9. CTA: one, specific, conversational, rotated
A single CTA (competing CTAs mean most people pick none). Be specific ("What's the one metric you track most closely?" beats "Thoughts?"). Avoid engagement bait. Vary the CTA type across posts so the closer does not go stale.
